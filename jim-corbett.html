India's oldest national park — Home to 260+ Royal Bengal Tigers, diverse wildlife, and the legendary Dhikala grasslands. Guided by Talib Hussain & Shahrukh Khan.

About Jim Corbett National Park

Jim Corbett National Park, established in 1936 as Hailey National Park, holds the prestigious distinction of being India's oldest national park and one of the most celebrated wildlife sanctuaries in all of Asia. Located in the foothills of the Himalayas in the Nainital and Pauri Garhwal districts of Uttarakhand, this iconic park was originally created to protect the critically endangered Bengal Tiger. Today, it stands as a testament to India's conservation legacy, drawing hundreds of thousands of wildlife enthusiasts, photographers, and nature lovers from across the globe every year.

The park was renamed in 1956 in honour of the legendary British-Indian hunter-turned-conservationist Colonel Jim Corbett, who played a pivotal role in its establishment and dedicated his life to protecting the region's wildlife. Corbett famously hunted man-eating tigers and leopards that terrorized the villages of Kumaon, but he later became one of the most vocal advocates for wildlife conservation in India. His books — Man-Eaters of Kumaon and The Temple Tiger — brought international attention to the region's incredible biodiversity. In 1973, Jim Corbett National Park became the first reserve under Project Tiger, the Government of India's landmark tiger conservation programme, making it the birthplace of organized tiger conservation in the country.

Spread across a massive 1,318.54 sq km (including the Sonanadi Wildlife Sanctuary buffer zone), the park's landscape is extraordinarily diverse — ranging from dense sal forests (Shorea robusta), dry deciduous woodlands, and sprawling grasslands to riverine belts along the majestic Ramganga River, which forms the lifeline of the entire ecosystem. The park is home to approximately 260+ Royal Bengal Tigers, giving it one of the highest tiger densities in India. Beyond tigers, the park shelters over 600 Asian Elephants, leopards, Himalayan black bears, sloth bears, gharials, and an astonishing 650+ bird species, making it a world-class destination for both mammal and avian enthusiasts.

Safari Zones in Jim Corbett

Jim Corbett National Park is divided into six distinct safari zones, each offering a unique landscape, wildlife composition, and experience. Zone selection is a critical decision when planning your Corbett safari, as permits are zone-specific and each area has its own opening season and regulations.

🏔️ Dhikala Zone

The crown jewel of Corbett. Dhikala is the largest and most iconic zone, famous for its expansive chaur (grasslands) that offer unobstructed views of tigers, elephants, and wild herds. An overnight stay at Dhikala Forest Lodge is mandatory, and visitors can enjoy both Jeep and Canter safaris. The Dhikala Canter Safari through the Ramganga reservoir area is a once-in-a-lifetime wildlife experience. Open: 15 Nov – 15 Jun.

🐅 Bijrani Zone

Most popular for day visitors. Bijrani offers an excellent mix of sal forests, open grasslands, and water bodies — creating ideal conditions for tiger sightings. This zone has one of the highest tiger sighting success rates and is easily accessible from Ramnagar. It's perfect for those who want a memorable Jeep Safari without an overnight stay inside the park. Open: 15 Nov – 15 Jun.

🐻 Jhirna Zone

Open year-round — making it the go-to zone during monsoon and early winter. Jhirna is renowned for frequent sightings of sloth bears, leopards, and wild elephants. Its diverse flora includes mixed deciduous and thorn forests, and the zone's relatively quieter trails make it a favourite among serious wildlife enthusiasts and photographers. Open: All year round.

🦚 Dhela Zone

Buffer zone with rich birdlife. Added to the safari circuit in 2014, Dhela is a buffer zone that offers a peaceful and less crowded alternative. It is especially popular among birdwatchers, with frequent sightings of peacocks, kingfishers, and various raptors. The lush vegetation and tranquil atmosphere make it ideal for nature walks and photography. Open: All year round.

🦅 Durga Devi Zone

Birdwatcher's paradise. Located along the northeastern boundary of the park, Durga Devi Zone features hilly terrain with dense forests along the Ramganga River. It is considered the best zone for birdwatching in Corbett, with species like the Maroon Oriole, Great Hornbill, and Crested Serpent Eagle. Fishing (catch-and-release) for Mahseer is also popular here. Open: 15 Nov – 15 Jun.

🌿 Sitabani Zone

No entry fee required. Sitabani is a buffer zone located outside the main tiger reserve and does not require a park entry permit. It is perfect for leisurely nature walks, birdwatching, and enjoying the serene forest atmosphere. While big cat sightings are less frequent, it is excellent for spotting deer, wild boar, and a wide variety of resident and migratory birds. Open: All year round.

Wildlife of Jim Corbett National Park

Jim Corbett National Park is one of the most biodiverse regions in the Indian subcontinent, harbouring an extraordinary range of fauna across its varied habitats — from dense sal forests and riverine woodlands to open grasslands and marshes.

🐅 Mammals

The park's star attraction is the Royal Bengal Tiger, with a population of over 260 individuals — one of the highest tiger densities anywhere in India. The vast Asian Elephant population exceeds 600 individuals, making herds a common and awe-inspiring sight, especially in the Dhikala grasslands. Other notable mammals include:

  • Leopard — elusive but present in good numbers across all zones
  • Himalayan Black Bear — found in the higher elevation areas and Durga Devi Zone
  • Sloth Bear — frequently spotted in Jhirna Zone, especially during summer
  • Wild Boar — abundant throughout the park, often seen in family groups
  • Sambar Deer — the largest deer species in India, commonly found near water bodies
  • Chital (Spotted Deer) — the most common deer species, found in large herds
  • Barking Deer (Muntjac) — small, shy deer found in dense undergrowth
  • Hog Deer — rare and found primarily in the grassland areas near Dhikala
  • Indian Pangolin — critically endangered and extremely rare to spot

🦅 Birds (650+ Species)

Corbett is a world-renowned birding destination with over 650 recorded species, including both resident and migratory birds. The park has been designated as an Important Bird Area (IBA) by BirdLife International. Notable species include:

  • Great Hornbill — the iconic large bird with its massive casque
  • Crested Serpent Eagle — a common raptor soaring above the canopy
  • Black-chinned Babbler — a Corbett speciality found in bamboo thickets
  • Hodgson's Bushchat — a winter visitor found in the grasslands
  • Wallcreeper, Pallas's Fish Eagle, Tawny Fish Owl, Brown Dipper and many more

🐊 Reptiles & Aquatic Life

The Ramganga River and its tributaries support a rich population of aquatic reptiles:

  • Gharial — the critically endangered fish-eating crocodilian found in the Ramganga reservoir
  • Mugger Crocodile — marsh crocodile found basking along river banks
  • King Cobra — the world's longest venomous snake, found in dense forest areas
  • Indian Python — a large constrictor found near water bodies and in rocky areas
  • Monitor Lizard — commonly seen sunning on rocks and tree trunks
  • Golden Mahseer — the prized game fish of the Ramganga, now a protected species

How to Reach Jim Corbett National Park

Jim Corbett National Park is one of the most accessible national parks in India, well-connected by road, rail, and air. The park's main entry gate at Amdanda is located approximately 12 km from Ramnagar town, the base camp for all Corbett safaris.

🚗 From Delhi (By Road)

Jim Corbett is 260 km from Delhi, accessible via NH9 through Ghaziabad → Hapur → Moradabad → Kashipur → Ramnagar. The drive takes approximately 6-7 hours depending on traffic. State transport and private Volvo buses also operate on this route daily.

🚂 Nearest Railway Station

Ramnagar Railway Station is just 12 km from the park entrance and is well-connected to Delhi, Moradabad, Lucknow, and other major cities. The overnight Ranikhet Express from Delhi Old Junction is the most popular train option among safari visitors.

✈️ Nearest Airport

Pantnagar Airport (PGH) is the closest airport, located approximately 80 km from Ramnagar. It has limited flights from Delhi. Alternatively, Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun (230 km) and Delhi IGI Airport (260 km) offer broader connectivity.

🏔️ From Nainital

If you're combining your Corbett safari with a Nainital hill station visit, the park is just 65 km (approximately 2 hours) from Nainital. The scenic drive passes through the Kumaon hills and dense forests, making it a delightful journey itself.

Best Time to Visit Jim Corbett

Choosing the right time to visit Jim Corbett dramatically affects your wildlife sighting experience. Here is a detailed seasonal breakdown to help you plan:

❄️ Winter (November – February)

Best season overall. Pleasant weather (8°C–25°C), excellent visibility, and animals frequently visit water bodies. This is peak season with maximum tiger and elephant sightings. All zones are open. Book well in advance as permits sell out fast.

☀️ Summer (March – June)

Best for tiger sighting. As the heat intensifies (25°C–40°C), the vegetation thins out and animals congregate around water sources, significantly increasing the probability of spotting tigers, elephants, and other large mammals. Morning safaris are highly recommended.

🌧️ Monsoon (July – October)

Most zones closed. Heavy rainfall causes flooding in the Ramganga valley. Only Jhirna and Dhela zones remain open. The park is lush and green, ideal for birdwatching and photography, but large mammal sightings are less predictable.

📅 Peak Season Tips

Book 45-60 days in advance for Dhikala and Bijrani zones. Weekend permits sell out within minutes of release. Weekday safaris offer better availability and a more peaceful experience. Plan multiple safari drives to maximize your sighting chances.

Accommodation Inside & Near Jim Corbett

🏕️ Forest Rest Houses (Inside the Park)

For the most immersive wildlife experience, the Uttarakhand Forest Department operates Forest Rest Houses (FRHs) inside the park at several locations:

  • Dhikala Forest Lodge — The most sought-after accommodation. Located deep inside the Dhikala Zone, it offers log huts, annexes, and a canteen with basic meals. Waking up to the sounds of the jungle and the sight of elephants grazing on the Dhikala chaur is an unforgettable experience.
  • Bijrani Forest Rest House — Located at the entrance of the Bijrani Zone, ideal for day-safari visitors who want to explore the zone early in the morning.
  • Gairal Forest Rest House — A secluded rest house on the banks of the Ramganga River, known for its peaceful ambiance and occasional tiger sightings from the veranda.
  • Sultan & Sarpduli — Remote rest houses for serious wildlife enthusiasts seeking solitude and raw jungle experience.

All Forest Rest House bookings must be made in advance through the official Corbett Tiger Reserve (CTR) website or through authorized tour operators. Availability is limited and high in demand, especially for Dhikala.

🏨 Private Resorts (Outside the Park)

For those seeking luxury and comfort, numerous private resorts and hotels are available in Ramnagar, Dhikuli, and along the Kosi River. These range from budget guesthouses to five-star luxury jungle lodges, many featuring pools, spas, and in-house naturalists. Popular resort areas include:

  • Dhikuli — The main resort corridor, just 2 km from the Bijrani gate
  • Ramnagar Market Area — Budget-friendly hotels with easy access to the railway station
  • Kosi River Belt — Premium riverside resorts with stunning views and eco-lodge concepts

Booking Tips & Important Rules

Navigating the Jim Corbett safari booking system can be tricky for first-time visitors. Here are essential tips and park rules to ensure a smooth and safe experience:

📋 Booking Guidelines

  • Book Early: Safari permits open 45 days in advance. Dhikala and Bijrani permits sell out within hours of release, especially for weekends and holidays.
  • Valid ID Required: Indian nationals need Aadhaar Card; foreign nationals need a valid Passport. The name on the booking must match the ID exactly.
  • Zone-Specific Permits: Each permit is valid for one specific zone and one specific time slot (morning or evening). You cannot switch zones.
  • Children Under 5: Free entry, but they still occupy a seat in the Gypsy and must be listed in the booking.
  • Cancellation Policy: Government bookings have strict cancellation policies. Refunds are partial and vary based on the cancellation timeline.

⚠️ Park Rules & Safety

  • Stay Inside the Vehicle: It is strictly prohibited to step out of the Gypsy or Canter during the safari for safety reasons.
  • No Loud Noise: Maintain silence during the safari. Loud music, shouting, and honking are prohibited.
  • No Plastic: Carrying plastic bags and bottles inside the park is banned. Use reusable water bottles.
  • Dress Code: Wear earthy, muted colours (olive, khaki, brown). Avoid bright colours and white clothing as they disturb wildlife.
  • No Smoking/Alcohol: Strictly prohibited inside the national park boundaries.
  • Follow Guide Instructions: Your assigned naturalist guide's instructions are mandatory. They ensure both your safety and the well-being of wildlife.
