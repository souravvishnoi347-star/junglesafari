About Rajaji National Park

Rajaji National Park is one of India's most ecologically significant national parks and tiger reserves, sprawling across 820 square kilometres in the Shivalik range of the western Himalayas. Straddling three districts — Haridwar, Dehradun, and Pauri Garhwal — in the state of Uttarakhand, the park represents the north-western limit of the tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forest biome. It was officially declared a national park on 15 September 1983 by merging three existing wildlife sanctuaries — Rajaji Sanctuary, Motichur Sanctuary, and Chilla Sanctuary — into a single protected area. The park is named after C. Rajagopalachari, affectionately known as Rajaji, who was the last Governor-General of independent India and a stalwart of the Indian independence movement.

What sets Rajaji National Park apart from other Indian wildlife reserves is its extraordinary Asian elephant population. With over 500 wild Asian elephants roaming its sal forests, riverine habitats, and grasslands, Rajaji harbours one of the largest and most genetically viable elephant herds in all of India. The park forms a critical segment of the Shivalik Elephant Reserve, which serves as a vital corridor for elephant migration between the Shivalik foothills and the Terai grasslands. In 2015, the park was granted Tiger Reserve status, further elevating its conservation importance and bringing in additional resources for habitat protection and anti-poaching operations.

Beyond elephants and tigers, Rajaji is a biodiversity hotspot that supports Royal Bengal Tigers (50+), Indian leopards, Himalayan black bears, sloth bears, king cobras, and an astounding array of over 315 bird species — making it a paradise for ornithologists and wildlife photographers alike. The park's terrain transitions dramatically from the Ganges River floodplains in the south to rugged Shivalik ridges in the north, creating a mosaic of habitats including dense sal forests, scrub jungles, riverine woodlands, and alpine meadows. Whether you're an ardent wildlife enthusiast, a serious birdwatcher, or a family seeking an unforgettable nature experience, a safari in Rajaji promises encounters with the wild that remain etched in memory forever.

Official Safari Zones & Gates in Rajaji National Park

Rajaji National Park and its contiguous protected reserves feature 6 official safari gates and tourism zones. Each zone offers a distinct landscape, terrain, and specialized wildlife sightings. The zone you choose shapes your safari experience. Here is the complete official gate order:

1

Chilla Zone

The flagship and most renowned safari zone of Rajaji Tiger Reserve, located along the eastern bank of the holy Ganges near Haridwar and Rishikesh (entry via Chilla Dam, Yamkeshwar). Spanning 148 sq km with a 36 km scenic jungle track, Chilla is celebrated for frequent daylight sightings of wild Asian elephant herds, Royal Bengal tigers, leopards, and spotted deer grazing across open riverine grasslands. Proximity to the Ganges makes it a premier hotspot for waterbirds.

2

Ranipur Zone Haridwar

Situated directly on the southwestern edge of Haridwar near the Shivalik foothills and BHEL sector. Known for rugged mountain terrain, natural seasonal streams (raus), and dry deciduous forests. A prime territory for Indian leopards, sambar deer, barking deer (kakad), wild boars, and over 250 species of birds including the Crested Serpent Eagle and Great Indian Hornbill. Its quiet, lower-footfall trails offer undisturbed and peaceful game drives.

3

Jhilmil Jhel Zone

A world-renowned marshland wetland and conservation reserve located just 15 km from Haridwar near Tantwala and the Rawasan river corridor. Jhilmil Jhel is celebrated as the exclusive home of the majestic Swamp Deer (Barasingha) in Uttarakhand. Vast grasslands and freshwater bodies also host wild elephant herds, leopards, nilgai, otters, and flocks of winter migratory waterfowl.

4

Chillawali Mohand Zone

Positioned along the historic Shivalik pass highway connecting Dehradun and Saharanpur. Chillawali Mohand features towering sal tree canopies, deep jungle gorges, and natural elephant migratory tracks. Renowned for raw wilderness, this zone provides extraordinary opportunities to spot wild Asian elephants, leopards, sloth bears, striped hyenas, and Shivalik forest wildlife far from heavy tourist circuits.

5

Vindhyawasini Zone

Located along the sacred Vindhyawasini forest belt near the Rawasan river valley in the eastern Shivalik buffer. Characterized by scenic hill slopes, dense mixed scrub, and crystal-clear forest streams. An excellent habitat for leopards, goral (mountain goats) on rocky outcrops, elephant family groups, and raptors. Ideal for nature lovers and photographers seeking serene, untamed trails.

6

Motichur Zone

Situated on the western bank of the Ganges near Raiwala along the Haridwar–Rishikesh corridor. Characterized by dense ancient sal forests, narrow ravines, and crucial elephant migratory passages connecting Rajaji's western and eastern sectors. Highly favored for stealthy Indian leopard sightings, barking deer, wild boar, and tranquil jungle drives under deep green tree canopies.

Best Time to Visit Rajaji National Park

Rajaji National Park is open for safari from 15 November to 15 June each year. The park closes during the monsoon season (mid-June to mid-November) due to heavy rainfall, swollen rivers, and the breeding season of wildlife. Each season within the open window offers a distinctly different safari experience:

🌿 Winter Season (November – February)

The most comfortable time to visit, with pleasant daytime temperatures of 15–25°C. Dense morning mist in the sal forests creates a magical atmosphere. This is the peak season for birdwatching, as migratory species from Central Asia — including several species of flycatchers, thrushes, and raptors — arrive in large numbers. Elephant herds are frequently seen near grasslands and river beds. Wildlife sighting success rate is high due to reduced foliage.

☀️ Summer Season (March – June)

Temperatures can rise to 35–42°C, but this is arguably the best time for big cat sightings. As water sources shrink, tigers, leopards, and other mammals congregate near remaining waterholes and streams, dramatically increasing sighting probability. The deciduous trees shed their leaves, opening up the forest canopy and making it easier to spot wildlife. Morning safaris are particularly productive. Carry sun protection, stay hydrated, and wear light-colored clothing.

Pro Tip: For the best overall experience combining comfortable weather with good wildlife sighting chances, we recommend visiting during late February to mid-April or in November when the park first opens.

Safari Booking Rules & Guidelines

To ensure a safe, respectful, and enjoyable wildlife experience for all visitors, Rajaji National Park enforces a set of rules that every safari participant must follow. Violation of these rules can result in fines, expulsion from the park, or legal action under the Wildlife Protection Act:

  • Carry Valid ID Proof: All visitors must carry a government-issued photo ID (Aadhaar, Passport, Driving License, or Voter ID). Foreign nationals must carry their passport and valid Indian visa. ID details provided during booking must match the physical document.
  • No Loud Music or Honking: Maintain silence throughout the safari. Loud noises disturb wildlife behavior, disrupt animal communication, and reduce sighting chances for everyone in the park. Mobile phones must be kept on silent or vibrate mode.
  • No Plastic Inside the Park: Single-use plastics — including bottles, bags, and wrappers — are strictly prohibited inside Rajaji National Park. Carry reusable water bottles and eco-friendly containers. Any litter must be brought back out of the park.
  • Stay Inside the Vehicle at All Times: Under no circumstances should visitors exit the safari vehicle during the drive. Standing on seats, leaning out of the vehicle, or climbing onto the roof is strictly prohibited for your safety and to avoid disturbing wildlife.
  • Follow the Guide's Instructions: Your assigned naturalist guide is a trained forest professional. Follow all their instructions regarding movement, photography distance, and behavior around wildlife. They have authority to modify or terminate the safari if safety is compromised.
