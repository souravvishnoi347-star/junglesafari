600+ Elephants

Rajaji National Park

Spread across 820 sq km along the Shivalik foothills, Rajaji Tiger Reserve is a biodiversity hotspot sheltering over 600 Asian elephants — one of the largest herds in northwestern India. The park features dense sal forests, riverine habitats along the Ganges and Song rivers, Indian leopards, and 400+ bird species.

Tiger Heartland

Jim Corbett National Park

Established in 1936 as India's first national park, Jim Corbett spans 1,318 sq km and boasts one of the highest Bengal tiger densities in the world. Renowned for the expansive Dhikala grasslands, Bijrani sal forests, Ramganga riverbed, and 260+ Royal Bengal Tigers.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

The best time for a jungle safari in Uttarakhand is from November to June. The winter months (November–February) offer excellent wildlife sightings as animals gather near water sources, and the cool weather makes the experience pleasant. Summer months (March–June) are ideal for tiger and elephant sightings since the sparse vegetation improves visibility significantly. Both Rajaji National Park and Jim Corbett National Park remain open during this period, though specific zones may have varying schedules. The parks are generally closed during the monsoon season (July–September) due to heavy rainfall, flooding, and to allow wildlife breeding and habitat regeneration.

You can book a safari in Rajaji National Park through our website or by contacting us directly via phone or WhatsApp. Online bookings can also be made through the official Uttarakhand Forest Department portal. You will need to provide a valid government-issued photo ID (Aadhaar, Passport, or Driving License) for all visitors. We recommend booking at least 2–3 days in advance, especially during weekends and holidays, as permits are limited per zone per day. Our team handles all permit formalities, park entry fees, guide arrangements, and vehicle booking so you can enjoy a completely hassle-free wildlife experience.

The Rajaji National Park safari cost varies depending on the type of safari and vehicle chosen. A Jeep Safari (Gypsy) typically costs between ₹3,000 to ₹5,500 per trip for up to 6 persons, which includes the vehicle, driver, park entry fee, and a trained naturalist guide. Canter Safari options are also available at approximately ₹1,500 per person in shared vehicles. Indian nationals pay lower park entry fees compared to foreign nationals. Additional charges may apply for camera fees and premium zone access. Our all-inclusive packages start at ₹3,500 per person, covering permit, gypsy, guide, refreshments, and complimentary pick-up/drop from Haridwar city.

Both parks offer unique and complementary wildlife experiences. Jim Corbett National Park, established in 1936 as India's first national park, is renowned for its exceptionally high density of Royal Bengal Tigers and offers the iconic Dhikala zone with sweeping grasslands and riverine habitats along the Ramganga. Rajaji National Park (now Rajaji Tiger Reserve) is celebrated for its massive Asian Elephant herds — one of the largest congregations in North India — along with leopards, spotted deer, sloth bears, and over 400 bird species. Rajaji is closer to Haridwar and Delhi (just 4.5 hours), making it ideal for weekend trips, while Corbett requires a longer stay for deep jungle exploration. For the ultimate experience, we recommend our combined tour package covering both parks.

Rajaji National Park is home to an extraordinary diversity of wildlife. The park's star attractions include herds of Asian Elephants (over 600 individuals), Royal Bengal Tigers, Indian Leopards, Himalayan Black Bears, Sloth Bears, and Spotted Deer (Chital). You may also encounter Sambar Deer, Barking Deer (Muntjac), Wild Boar, Indian Pangolin, King Cobra, Monitor Lizards, and Gharials in the river sections. The park is a birdwatcher's paradise with over 400 documented species including the Great Pied Hornbill, Crested Serpent Eagle, Pallas's Fish Eagle, and various species of woodpeckers, kingfishers, and flycatchers. The Shivalik hills and Ganges riverbed create diverse microhabitats supporting this remarkably rich biodiversity.

No, Rajaji National Park is generally closed during the monsoon season from July 1st to October 15th (dates may vary slightly each year as per forest department notifications). The closure is necessary to allow wildlife breeding, habitat regeneration, and because heavy monsoon rainfall makes forest roads unsafe and impassable. The Ganges and Song rivers that flow through the park swell significantly during this period. Similarly, most zones in Jim Corbett National Park are closed during monsoon, with the notable exception of the Jhirna zone, which remains open year-round. We strongly recommend planning your visit between November and June for the best wildlife sighting opportunities and safest conditions.

Rajaji National Park is conveniently located approximately 220 km from Delhi, making it one of the most accessible national parks from the capital. By road, you can drive via NH-58 (Delhi–Meerut Expressway to Haridwar) in approximately 4.5–5 hours. The nearest railway station is Haridwar Junction (just 5 km from the Chilla gate), with frequent trains from Delhi including the Shatabdi Express, Jan Shatabdi Express, and Mussoorie Express. The nearest airport is Jolly Grant Airport in Dehradun (about 35 km away), connected to Delhi by daily flights on major airlines. Our safari packages include complimentary pick-up and drop from Haridwar railway station, Dehradun airport, or your hotel in the region.

Jim Corbett National Park has five distinct safari zones, each offering unique terrain and wildlife encounters. Dhikala Zone is the most prestigious and largest zone, famous for its vast chaur grasslands and the highest tiger sighting probability — permits are limited and must be booked well in advance. Bijrani Zone offers excellent tiger and elephant sightings with dense sal forests and is popular for day visitors. Jhirna Zone is open year-round (even during monsoon) and is known for peacock, elephant, and leopard sightings in its dry deciduous landscape. Durga Devi Zone borders the Ramganga River in the northeast and is a haven for birdwatchers and mahseer fishing enthusiasts. Sitabani Zone is a buffer area that does not require a forest permit and offers peaceful nature walks amidst ancient temple ruins and light forest cover.
